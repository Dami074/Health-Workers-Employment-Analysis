# 2025 Analysis of Health Workers Employment in Chikun Local Govt
### Toolset: Excel, Power Pivot, DAX, Data Modeling

## Project Overview
### The Chikun Local Government Area initiated a health workforce analysis project in 2025 to assess the distribution, demographics, and employment outcomes of health worker applicants across its 53 healthcare facilities and 26 wards. The goal was to identify staffing gaps, gender representation, and role-based applicant trends to inform future recruitment strategies and resource allocation. This dashboard was developed to visualize key metrics and support data-driven decision-making.

## Key Techniques
### •	Pivot Tables for dynamic aggregation of applicant data by role, gender, and facility.
### •	Conditional Formatting to highlight facilities with lowest and highest employee counts.
### •	Charts (Pie, Bar, Column) to visualize employment status, gender distribution, and role-based applications.
### •	Data Validation and Lookup functions to ensure consistency and accuracy.
### •	Dashboard design principles including color coding, layout optimization, and interactive slicers for user-friendly navigation.

## Executive Summary
### The dashboard presents a comprehensive overview of health worker employment in Chikun LGA for 2025. Out of 1,251 applicants, 86% were selected, with males comprising 66% of the employed workforce. The average age of applicants was 35 years. Staffing levels varied significantly across facilities, with some clinics having as few as one employee while others had up to 77. The most applied roles were Recorder and Dispenser, accounting for over two-thirds of total applications.

## Insights
### •	Employment Rate: High selection rate (86%) suggests a strong demand for health workers or effective applicant screening.
### •	Gender Disparity: Females dominate the workforce (66%), indicating potential gender imbalance in recruitment or application rates.
### •	Facility Staffing Gaps:
#### o	10 facilities have critically low staffing (≤5 employees), which may impact service delivery.
#### o	10 facilities are heavily staffed (≥39 employees), possibly serving larger populations or acting as central hubs.
### •	Role Popularity:
#### o	Recorder (435) and Dispenser (415) roles are most sought after.
#### o	Supervisory and administrative roles have significantly fewer applicants, suggesting a need for targeted recruitment.
### •	Geographic Spread: With 53 facilities across 26 wards, staffing distribution is uneven and may reflect population density or infrastructure disparities.

## Recommendations
### •	Targeted Recruitment: Focus on underrepresented roles like Health Facility Supervisors and OICs to balance operational capacity.
### •	Gender Inclusion Programs: Encourage female participation through outreach and incentives to improve gender equity.
### •	Redistribution Strategy: Reallocate staff from overstaffed to understaffed facilities where feasible.
### •	Facility-Level Audits: Conduct assessments to understand why certain clinics have minimal staffing and address underlying issues.
### •	Training & Development: Invest in capacity building for less popular roles to attract more qualified applicants.


## Limitations
### •	Data Scope: The dashboard is limited to 2025 applicants and does not include historical trends or future projections.
### •	Demographic Depth: Beyond age and gender, other demographic factors (education, experience) were not analyzed.
### •	Facility Performance Metrics: Staffing levels are shown, but no data on facility performance or patient outcomes is included.
